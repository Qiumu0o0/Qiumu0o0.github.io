# DK-STP (Dimension-Keeping Semi-Tensor Product) - 1

## Classical STP

### MM-STP

Let $A \in \mathcal{M}_{m\times n}$, $B \in \mathcal{M}_{p\times q}$, and $t=\operatorname{lcm}(n,p)$. Define

$$
A\ltimes B=(A\otimes I_{t/n})(B\otimes I_{t/p}).
$$

The dimensions are

$$
(m\times n)\otimes(t/n\times t/n)=(mt/n\times t),
$$

$$
(p\times q)\otimes(t/p\times t/p)=(t\times qt/p).
$$

Hence, $A\ltimes B\in\mathcal{M}_{mt/n\times qt/p}$.

### MV-STP

Let $A\in\mathcal{M}_{m\times n}$, $x\in\mathbb{R}^p$, and $t=\operatorname{lcm}(n,p)$. Define

$$
A\mathop{\ltimes}\limits^{\rightarrow}x=(A\otimes I_{t/n})(x\otimes\mathbf{1}_{t/p}).
$$

The dimensions are

$$
(m\times n)\otimes(t/n\times t/n)=(mt/n\times t),
$$

$$
(p\times1)\otimes(t/p\times1)=(t\times1).
$$

Therefore,

$$
(mt/n\times t)(t\times1)=(mt/n\times1).
$$

Hence, $A\mathop{\ltimes}\limits^{\rightarrow}x\in\mathbb{R}^{mt/n}$.

### VV-STP

Let $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$, $y\in\mathbb{R}^p$, and $t=\operatorname{lcm}(n,p)$. Their semi-tensor product is

$$
x\mathop{\ltimes}\limits^{\rightarrow}y
=(x\otimes\mathbf{1}_{t/n})^{\mathsf T}(y\otimes\mathbf{1}_{t/p}).
$$

The dimension calculations are

$$
(1\times n)\otimes(1\times t/n)=(1\times t),
$$

$$
(p\times1)\otimes(t/p\times1)=(t\times1).
$$

Therefore,

$$
(1\times t)(t\times1)=(1\times1).
$$

Hence, $x\mathop{\ltimes}\limits^{\rightarrow}y\in\mathbb{R}$.

These are the three classical forms of STP.

>P.S. The semi-tensor product was introduced to extend matrix multiplication. When the dimensions of two matrices are incompatible with ordinary matrix multiplication, extension multipliers are used to make their inner dimensions compatible.

### Questions

>Are MV-STP and VV-STP special cases of MM-STP?

No. MV-STP and VV-STP are not direct special cases of MM-STP under these definitions. MM-STP uses identity matrices as extension multipliers, whereas the vector-involved cases use **all-ones vectors**. VV-STP also transposes the first extended vector.

>If not, why? Give a concrete example by assigning specific values in the MM-STP case to illustrate the differences from the MV-STP and VV-STP cases.

For MV-STP, set

$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\end{bmatrix},\qquad
x=\begin{bmatrix}3&4&5\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T},\qquad t=6.
$$

Treating $x$ as a $3\times1$ matrix, MM-STP gives

$$
(A\otimes I_3)(x\otimes I_2)
=\begin{bmatrix}3&8\\10&3\\4&10\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times2}.
$$

In contrast, MV-STP gives

$$
(A\otimes I_3)(x\otimes\mathbf{1}_2)
=\begin{bmatrix}11\\13\\14\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.
$$

## DK-STP

Let $A\in\mathcal{M}_{m\times n}$, $B\in\mathcal{M}_{p\times q}$, and $t=\operatorname{lcm}(n,p)$. The **dimension-keeping semi-tensor product** is defined by

$$
A\mathbin{\varowedge}B
=(A\otimes\mathbf{1}_{t/n}^{\mathsf T})(B\otimes\mathbf{1}_{t/p}).
$$

The dimensions of the two extended components are

$$
(m\times n)\otimes(1\times t/n)=(m\times t),
$$

$$
(p\times q)\otimes(t/p\times1)=(t\times q).
$$

Therefore,

$$
(m\times t)(t\times q)=(m\times q).
$$

Hence,

$$
A\mathbin{\varowedge}B\in\mathcal{M}_{m\times q}.
$$

The operation is called *dimension-keeping* because its result always keeps the row dimension of $A$ and the column dimension of $B$. In particular, if $A,B\in\mathcal{M}_{m\times n}$, then

$$
A\mathbin{\varowedge}B\in\mathcal{M}_{m\times n}.
$$

When $n=p$, both extension multipliers reduce to $\mathbf{1}_1=[1]$, so DK-STP coincides with the ordinary matrix product $AB$.

>P.S. In terms of the dimensions of the result, DK-STP is more similar to classical matrix multiplication.

### Interpretation of the Three Products

For the classical matrix product $AB$, **it represents the composition of two compatible linear transformations.** In applications, it can also be interpreted as weighted aggregation: the entries of a row provide weights for combining the corresponding entries of a column.

### Weighted DK-STP

For each $k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}$, define the **normalized all-ones vector**

$$
\varepsilon_k:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\mathbf{1}_k\in\mathbb{R}^k.
$$

Let $A\in\mathcal{M}_{m\times n}$, $B\in\mathcal{M}_{p\times q}$, and $t=\operatorname{lcm}(n,p)$. The **weighted dimension-keeping semi-tensor product** is defined by

$$
A\mathbin{\varowedge}_{w}B
=(A\otimes\varepsilon_{t/n}^{\mathsf T})(B\otimes\varepsilon_{t/p}).
$$

The dimensions of the two extended components are

$$
(m\times n)\otimes(1\times t/n)=(m\times t),
$$

$$
(p\times q)\otimes(t/p\times1)=(t\times q).
$$

Therefore,

$$
(m\times t)(t\times q)=(m\times q),
$$

and hence

$$
A\mathbin{\varowedge}_{w}B\in\mathcal{M}_{m\times q}.
$$

Since

$$
\varepsilon_{t/n}=\sqrt{\frac{n}{t}}\mathbf{1}_{t/n},
\qquad
\varepsilon_{t/p}=\sqrt{\frac{p}{t}}\mathbf{1}_{t/p},
$$

the weighted and unweighted versions satisfy

$$
A\mathbin{\varowedge}_{w}B
=\frac{\sqrt{np}}{t}\left(A\mathbin{\varowedge}B\right).
$$

The normalization gives each extension multiplier unit Euclidean norm. When $n=p$, $t=n=p$ and the weighted DK-STP reduces to the ordinary matrix product $AB$.

>Is weighted DK-STP a special case of DK-STP?

No. Weighted DK-STP is a normalized variant of DK-STP rather than a special case. Their relationship is

$$
A\mathbin{\varowedge}_{w}B
=\frac{\sqrt{np}}{t}\left(A\mathbin{\varowedge}B\right).
$$

In general, they differ by the normalization factor $\sqrt{np}/t$. They coincide only when $n=p$, for which $t=n=p$ and $\sqrt{np}/t=1$.
